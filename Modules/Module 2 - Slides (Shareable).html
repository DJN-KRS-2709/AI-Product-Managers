Provocation

AI is a bet, not a feature.

Vote, then we unpack with real cases.
TRUE
"Most AI features die before product-market fit because they were fake-good."
Demos brilliantly · no clear value frame · loses to a platform feature in 6 months. The four-frame filter catches them early.
FALSE
"If we ship it and users use it, the bet was right."
Usage ≠ value. Notion AI tracked usage, then audited which of those uses survived a 3× cost stress test.
PARTIAL
"PMs decide autonomy levels."
PMs should. Most teams default autonomy to whatever engineering ships. That's a strategic gap.

Takeaway

Autonomy is a strategic decision. Don't default it.

Lecture

Fake-Good vs Boring Killer

Two simple filters keep a quarter from disappearing.
Fake-Good
Demos brilliantly.
No clear value frame · loses to a platform feature in 6 months · "Generate this summary."
Boring Killer
Looks dull on a slide.
Ladders to a real value frame · survives a 3× cost stress test · "Cut on-call escalations 40%."

Two-question filter: (a) does it ladder to a value frame? (b) survives a 3× cost stress? If either no — it's fake-good.

Takeaway

Two questions kill 80% of bad AI bets in 60 seconds.

Framework

The Four AI Value Propositions

If your bet doesn't ladder to one of these — it's fake-good.
💸
Cost
Replace human labour. Lower OPEX per unit of work.
📈
Revenue
New product surfaces, new willingness to pay.
🛡
Risk
Catch what humans miss — compliance, fraud, escalation.
✨
Delight
Anticipate intent, reduce friction, surprise positively.

For Juno: Risk is primary. Cost is secondary. Revenue and Delight come later.

Takeaway

Pick the primary frame. That's the frame the CFO will probe.

Framework

Suggest → Draft → Execute → Operate

As autonomy rises, error cost skyrockets. High-risk jobs cap at Draft.
①
Suggest
"Here's an idea." Human decides. Lowest risk, lowest leverage.
②
Draft
"Here's a v0.1." Human edits + approves. Most AI PM work lives here.
③
Execute
AI acts; human reviews after. Requires explicit guardrails.
④
Operate
AI runs long horizons. Requires explicit handoff rules (M5).

For Juno: Synthesise = Execute · Draft = Draft · Prioritise = Suggest, then Draft once trust is earned.

Takeaway

Pick the lowest level that delivers the job.

Framework

AI Solution Decision Matrix

Pressure-test every proposed AI bet on five axes, scored 1–5.
AxisQuestion1 (red flag)5 (boring killer)
Value clarityDoes it ladder to a value frame?VagueQuantified $$$ or $$ saved
Technical feasibilityCan current models do this well enough?Novel researchStandard pattern, off-the-shelf
Data availabilityDo you have / can you get the corpus?No corpusClean labelled corpus exists
Risk toleranceCan you tolerate wrong outputs?Regulatory / reputationalInternal, reversible
Strategic fitDoes it earn company oxygen?Pet projectDirectly serves a top-3 priority

Score < 3 average = don't ship. Score 4+ on all five = boring killer.

Case Study

Klarna's AI Assistant

The Bet
Klarna · Replace 700 customer-service reps with a single LLM-powered agent.
The Crack
Headlines say 700 jobs replaced. Real story = handoff rate. Some queries still escalate to humans — and which ones matters more than the percentage.
The Correction
Bet survives: agent for low-risk · human for novel/high-stakes · explicit boundary contract.

Lesson: the boundary between AI and human is the product.

Takeaway

The handoff rule, not the automation rate, is what makes the product safe to ship.
