# Module 2 — Glossary

Module-specific terms introduced in M2. See `Glossary.md` for course-wide terms.

---

**AI Solution Decision Matrix.** Five-axis scoring tool used to pressure-test an AI bet: Value clarity, Technical feasibility, Data availability, Risk tolerance, Strategic fit.

**AI Strategy One-Pager.** A seven-block one-page artifact justifying an AI bet to leadership.

**Autonomy levels.** The four-step continuum: Suggest → Draft → Execute → Operate.

**Bet.** The unit of AI product work. Replaces "feature" in AI-native PM, because bets fail more often than they succeed.

**Boring killer.** A feature that demos dull but ladders cleanly to a real value frame and survives a 3x cost stress test. The opposite of a fake-good.

**Compliance (Responsible AI bucket).** Regulatory exposure — GDPR, EU AI Act, sector-specific rules.

**Company oxygen.** The fifth axis of the Decision Matrix — whether a bet earns its way onto the top-3 priority list.

**Cost-of-wrong.** The Risk axis in Jobs × Risk × Autonomy — what bad output costs in reputation, regulation, revenue.

**Cost stress test (3x).** A filter: would this bet survive if inference costs tripled? Used to separate fake-goods from boring killers.

**Draft (autonomy level).** The AI produces a v0.1; a human edits and approves before anything ships. Most AI PM work lives here.

**Execute (autonomy level).** The AI acts on the world; a human reviews after the fact. Requires explicit guardrails.

**Fake-good.** A feature that demos brilliantly but doesn't ladder to a real value frame and dies under cost stress.

**Four AI Value Propositions.** Cost, Revenue, Risk, Delight.

**Implementation layer.** The third layer of the Three-Layer Model — prompt / RAG / fine-tune / agent.

**Jobs × Risk × Autonomy.** The strategy lens for choosing autonomy levels.

**Mechanic layer.** The middle layer of the Three-Layer Model — what the product actually does (the user-facing behaviour).

**Operate (autonomy level).** The highest autonomy — the AI runs over long horizons without check-ins. Requires explicit handoff rules (M5).

**Pressure-test.** Running an AI bet through the Decision Matrix before committing engineering oxygen to it.

**Reliability (Responsible AI bucket).** Uptime, fallback paths, graceful degradation when the model is down or wrong.

**Reputation (Responsible AI bucket).** Public failure scenarios and the response playbook for each.

**Responsible AI.** The strategic guardrails — Compliance + Safety + Reliability + Reputation.

**Risk mitigation (value frame).** One of the four AI Value Propositions — catching what humans miss.

**Safety (Responsible AI bucket).** Content moderation, prompt injection defense, misuse prevention.

**Score 4+ rule.** A bet that scores 4 or higher on all five Decision Matrix axes is a boring killer; ship it.

**Score below 3 rule.** A bet with an average score below 3 doesn't ship until the gap is closed.

**Strategic fit.** The fifth Decision Matrix axis — whether the bet directly serves a top-3 org priority.

**Strategy layer.** The top layer of the Three-Layer Model — the bet, the value frame, the why-now.

**Suggest (autonomy level).** The lowest autonomy — AI proposes, human decides. Used for high-risk jobs or when trust isn't yet earned.

**Three-Layer Model.** Strategy / Mechanic / Implementation. PMs own all three but most fail at Mechanic.

**Two-question filter.** The fake-good/boring-killer check: (a) value frame? (b) survives 3x cost stress?

**Value clarity.** The first Decision Matrix axis — whether the bet ladders to a quantified outcome.

**Value frame.** One of the Four AI Value Propositions (Cost, Revenue, Risk, Delight) — the ladder a bet must climb to.
