The traditional assumption M2 breaks

"Feature lists are roadmaps"

Traditional PM ranks features by ICE / RICE / WSJF and ships. In AI:

  • The same feature can be a *fake-good* (looks great in demos, dies in production) or a *boring killer* (looks dull, prints money).
  • The right autonomy level for a feature is a strategic decision, not an engineering one.
  • Bets fail more often than they succeed — your job is to kill bad bets fast and cheap.

You don't have feature lists. You have bets.

Reference the M1 mindset table — this module breaks the "spec is a doc" assumption. M2's spec is a *scored bet*.
Where Does AI Actually Fit?

Fake-Good vs. Boring Killer

Fake-Good

  • Demos brilliantly
  • No clear value frame
  • Loses to a platform feature in 6 months
  • "Generate this summary"

Boring Killer

  • Looks dull on a slide
  • Ladders to a real value frame
  • Survives a 3x cost stress test
  • "Cut on-call escalations by 40%"

Speaker Notes

Two-question filter: (a) does this ladder to a real value frame? (b) would it survive a 3x cost stress test? If either no, it's a fake-good.
The Four AI Value Propositions

Four ways AI creates real value

Cost

Replace human labour. Lower OPEX per unit of work.

Revenue

New product surfaces, new willingness to pay.

Risk

Catch what humans miss — compliance, fraud, escalation.

Delight

Anticipate intent, reduce friction, surprise positively.

If your AI bet doesn't ladder cleanly to one of these, it's a fake-good.

Speaker Notes

Force learners to label Juno's bet by frame. Most will land on Cost + Risk. Push them to specify which one is primary — the one that justifies the budget.

Balancing AI Bets with Autonomy

Jobs × Risk × Autonomy

The strategy lens for choosing how independently an AI feature can act.

AxisQuestion
JobWhat task is the AI doing?
RiskWhat's the cost of a wrong output? (reputation, regulation, revenue)
AutonomyHow independently can it act? Suggest → Draft → Execute → Operate

As autonomy increases, the cost of an error skyrockets. High-risk jobs cap at Draft.

Speaker Notes

Push them: should Juno draft a P0 spec without human oversight? (No — high-risk = stays at Draft, requires PM approval.) Should Juno summarise a backlog? (Yes — low-risk = can Execute.)
The Autonomy Levels

Suggest → Draft → Execute → Operate

Suggest

"Here's an idea." Human decides.

Lowest risk, lowest leverage.

Draft

"Here's a v0.1." Human edits + approves.

Most AI PM work lives here.

Execute

AI acts; human reviews after.

Requires explicit guardrails.

Operate

AI runs long horizons.

Requires explicit handoff rules (M5).

Speaker Notes

For Juno: Synthesize = Execute (low-risk, high-volume). Draft Specs = Draft (medium-risk, must be PM-edited). Prioritize Risks = Suggest at first, Draft once trust is built.

Making Product Decisions Based on Technical Needs

The AI Solution Decision Matrix

Pressure-test every proposed AI bet on five axes, scored 1–5.

AxisQuestion1 (red flag)5 (boring killer)
Value clarityDoes it ladder to a value frame?VagueQuantified $$$ or $$ saved
Technical feasibilityCan current models actually do this well enough?Novel researchStandard pattern, off-the-shelf
Data availabilityDo you have / can you get the corpus?No corpusClean labelled corpus exists
Risk toleranceCan you tolerate wrong outputs?Regulatory / reputationalInternal, reversible

Score <3 average = don't ship. Score 4+ on all five = boring killer.

Responsible AI: Strategic Guardrails

Risk is a strategic lever, not a checklist

  • Compliance — regulatory (GDPR, EU AI Act, sector-specific).
  • Safety — content, prompt injection, misuse.
  • Reliability — uptime, fallback paths, graceful degradation.
  • Reputation — public failure scenarios + response playbook.

Each guardrail you commit to is a sales asset for enterprise buyers — not just a cost.

The AI Strategy One-Pager

How to communicate a bet to leadership

Seven blocks. One page. The deliverable for M2.

  1. The bet — one sentence, with the value frame it ladders to.
  2. The user / job — who, what.
  3. The mechanic — what the product actually does.
  4. The autonomy — Suggest / Draft / Execute / Operate.
  5. The technical approach — prompt / RAG / fine-tune / agent.
  6. The risk + guardrail — what can go wrong + how you block it.
  7. The success metric — how you'll know it works.

Speaker Notes

Focus their feedback on the *justification phase*. The PM job is not to make a decision; it's to explain why this bet deserves company oxygen.

Self-review checklist

Before you ship the one-pager

  • The bet is one sentence, naming a value frame and a quantified outcome.
  • The mechanic answers "what does this product do?" in a way a non-PM understands.
  • The autonomy level is justified against Job × Risk.
  • The technical approach ladders to the M1 Optimization Framework (prompt → RAG → fine-tune).
  • The success metric is measurable in the first 30 days.

Speaker Notes

Hit the success metric hard. Most learners write vague "increase efficiency." Push them to "cut median P0 triage time from 90 min to 20 min by Sprint 3."
