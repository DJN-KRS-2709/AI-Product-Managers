Section 01
Production-Grade Evaluation Requirements

Speaker Notes

Section 1 — from vibe checks to systematic measurement.

Framework

Why “vibe checks” fail in production

A successful demo is not a quality bar. Three reasons evals are non-negotiable.
01
💡
Invisible Failures

A single successful output proves an AI can perform a task — not that it will reliably across 1,000 different intents.

Why it hurts

Without systematic measurement, high-stakes hallucinations and edge cases stay invisible until they reach the end user.

02
👁️
The Subjectivity Trap

Relying on “does this look good?” creates a vibe-check culture that can’t scale or provide objective data.

Why it hurts

There’s no defensible Go / No-Go signal — just opinions in a meeting room.

03
🌊
The Performance Gap

Trust is earned through consistent, measurable performance in a live environment.

Why it hurts

Not through a curated set of successful examples shown in a demo.

Speaker Notes

Source slide 6. Three reasons vibe checks fail: Invisible Failures, Subjectivity Trap, Performance Gap.

Mental model
The gap between lab and reality

The 95% accuracy trap

Lab metrics measure the model. Evals measure the product.
01
🧪
Accuracy · Precision · Recall · F1

Standard benchmarks are excellent for static datasets in controlled environments — but blind to the nuanced failures in user experience and trust.

Maths example

1,000 calls/day × 5% wrong = 50 wrong outputs/day. Some silent. One high-stakes wrong outweighs the other 950.

02
📝
User experience · Trust · Safety

Evals look beyond lab scores to measure the specific friction — latency, bias, inconsistency — that leads to product failure and loss of trust.

Product example

A “95% accurate” medical bot still fails if the 5% includes a wrong dosage recommendation.

PM rule: never quote model accuracy without quoting the eval bar next to it. “95% accurate at extracting risks” means nothing without the failure taxonomy of the missing 5%.

Section 02
Measuring AI-Powered Product Outputs

Speaker Notes

Section 2 — the AI Eval Stack and the PM’s role in defining quality.

Framework

The AI Eval Stack

Three layers. Always all three. They answer different questions.
03 · Online evals

User feedback. The final reality check — regeneration rates, task completion, thumbs — confirms the product is delivering value.

02 · System-level evals

Human evals. Where experts use structured rubrics to judge nuance, tone, and helpfulness — and provide the calibration the rest of the system depends on.

01 · Component-level evals

Automated assessment. Machine-detectable tests that filter obvious failures, check formatting, and measure speed and accuracy at scale.

PM oversight: a multi-layered evaluation plan balances speed, cost, and depth of insight. Skip a layer and you skip a class of failure.

Real-world example

Human evaluation at Google Assistant

A concrete reference implementation of the AI Eval Stack’s human-eval layer.
01
Context

Google was building a human-in-the-loop personal assistant product that combined humans and AI to fulfil complex user tasks.

02
Goal

They needed to assess task-fulfilment quality — quantify how the product delivered real-world value.

03
Method

The rubric consisted of 26 questions and included simple technical criteria, specific desired outcomes, and subjective human evaluations.

04
Training

The rubric was calibrated across Product, UX, and UXR — then used to train a dedicated team of manual reviewers.

05
Assessment

After each conversation, the transcript was sent to a reviewer to evaluate the system’s end-to-end performance.

06
Outcome

The data identified specific failure modes, allowing the team to eliminate launch blockers and improve the experience.

PM lift-out: they ran human evals on every conversation — not a sample. For your scale, sample with stratification (high / mid / low confidence).

PMs own the bar. Engineering runs the harness. QA can’t.
01
📋
Drafting the Definition of Quality

PM ownership

You own the gold standard

02
🚦
Setting the Pass Thresholds

You work with engineering to determine which failure modes are minor annoyances and which are launch blockers that stop a release.

PM ownership

You decide the acceptability gap

03
⚖️
Auditing the Automated Judges

You periodically audit the automated and “LLM-as-a-judge” layers to ensure the machine’s version of quality hasn’t drifted away from human reality.

PM ownership

You provide the ground truth

Section 3 — when evals signal a gap, here’s how PMs turn signal into action.

Framework

Operationalising and measuring AI risks

Use these performance signals as your dashboard to monitor live AI product quality and safety.
01
🚫
Blocked Request Rate

The frequency at which automated safety filters trigger to stop a response.

Measurement

Total Denied Prompts / Total User Inputs

Example

Finding that 15% of finance queries are being blocked, indicating your guardrails are too sensitive and hurting the UX.

02
📊
Hallucination Rate

The percentage of responses containing factually incorrect or ungrounded claims.

Measurement

Total Hallucinations / Total Responses

Example

Monitoring if a medical bot’s error rate rises above 1% triggers an immediate rollback to a previous version.

03
✍️
Human Override Rate

The rate at which a human-in-the-loop must correct the AI before the user sees the output.

Measurement

Total Human Edits / Total AI Outputs

Example

A customer-service tool where experts rewrite 40% of AI drafts signals the model isn’t saving time yet.

04
📉
Model Drift & Latency

The monitoring of performance degradation and response speed over time.

Measurement

Change in Accuracy + P99 Latency

Example

Identifying that a model has become 10% less accurate or 5s slower after a vendor updated the underlying LLM.

Speaker Notes

Source slide 15. Three live KPIs: Hallucination Rate, Human Override Rate, Model Drift & Latency. These are your dashboard.

Strategic logic

The governance framework

Four governance strategies that turn evals into production guardrails.
01
Hard vs. Soft Gates

Determine which failures must automatically block a release versus those that only require a manual PM sign-off.

Example

A safety failure triggers an automatic block; high latency only requires a PM justification to release.

02
Zero-Tolerance Metrics

For hard gates, establish the specific numerical thresholds that trigger an immediate production block.

Example

0% PII leakage to ensure the system never reveals sensitive data like employee salaries.

03
Grounding for Trust

Define specific output requirements that provide users with evidence of the AI’s reasoning.

Example

The model must provide direct citations or links to the HR policy it used to answer the question.

04
Build a Failure Taxonomy

Categorise potential AI failures into a clear document to align technical and business stakeholders on risk priority.

Example

A severity vs. frequency map prioritises fixing hallucinations in vacation policies over minor “off-brand” tone issues.

Dive deeper into each governance strategy in Product School’s AI Evals Certification.

Framework

Three levers to optimise AI product performance

When evaluations signal a quality gap, these are the three levers you pull.
01
✍️
The Prompt

Adjust the instructions or formatting to influence the AI’s output.

Trade-off axis

Behavior & Logic

Try

Few-shot, chain-of-thought prompting, or adding delimiters to structure your data.

02
🤖
The Model

Swap models or versions to find the best balance of quality, speed, and price.

Trade-off axis

Intelligence & Cost

Try

Comparing Gemini Pro vs. Flash for different task types or adjusting hyperparameters.

03
📚
The Data

Refine the information the AI accesses to ensure relevant and accurate responses.

Trade-off axis

Knowledge & Accuracy

Try

Cleaning the RAG dataset, adding more diverse data points, or fine-tuning the model.

PM rule of thumb: try Prompt → Model → Data. Architecture last. Most teams reach for architecture first — it’s the slowest and most expensive lever.

Source slide 17. Three levers: Prompt · Model · Data. PM rule of thumb: try prompt first, model next, data last. Architecture is the slowest, most expensive lever.

Framework

The PM execution plan

Four steps that turn evals into a production decision — with the trade-off named on each one.
1
Build Your Eval Plan

Define exactly what to measure, how often to test, and assign a clear quality owner for the final score.

• Key Decision: Speed vs. Certainty
2
Set Your Gatekeepers

Compare systematic results against your gold standard. Reject any launch based on a “good demo” if the data fails the bar.

• Key Decision: Brand Protection vs. Hype
3
Add Production Guardrails

Determine when the system must block a response, degrade to a safe script, or escalate to a human reviewer.

• Key Decision: Safety vs. Utility
4
Evolve Your Roadmap

Use performance gaps to decide whether to pivot your strategy or update your roadmap with new goals.

• Key Decision: Feature vs. Data

Key Takeaways

Measure AI Quality with Evals and Guardrails

Replace vibe checks with systematic evaluation harnesses. PMs catch invisible failures and make objective, data-driven launch decisions across diverse user intents.

Define hard gates with zero-tolerance metrics + a failure taxonomy. Establishing sensitivity thresholds protects the brand by balancing safety against necessary product utility.

Optimise via three levers: Prompt, Model, Data. Identify whether to refine instructions, swap models, or clean RAG datasets — targeted system improvement, in that order.
