Production-Grade Evaluation Requirements

Why vibe checks fail

"It looks good in five prompts" is the most common AI-PM trap.

  • A handful of successful prompts is not a quality bar.
  • Production traffic exposes long-tail inputs no demo ever touches.
  • Models drift; prompts decay; corpora go stale.
  • Without a harness, you can't tell whether your change made things better.

Vibe checks are the QA strategy of a team that has not shipped yet.

Speaker Notes

If anyone is currently in "demos look great" mode, this is the slide that should land.
The 95% Accuracy Trap

The last 5% is where production risk lives

95% accuracy on a golden set sounds great. Until you do the math at production volume:

  • 1,000 P0 thread triages per day × 5% wrong = 50 wrong outputs per day.
  • Some of those 50 are silently wrong (no PM correction).
  • A single high-stakes wrong output can outweigh the value of the other 950.

Eval design is the product surface that decides whether you can ship.

Speaker Notes

Reframe: a 95% pass rate is a 5% liability surface. The eval stack's job is to make that 5% visible and bounded.
The AI Evals Stack

Three layers — always all three

LayerVolumeFidelityWhat it tells you
User feedbackHighestLowestWhat users do (thumbs, regen, abandon)
Human evaluationMidHighWhat graders think (rubric scores)
Automated evalsHighestMidWhat an LLM-judge or regression suite measures

You need all three. They answer different questions.

Speaker Notes

Most teams ship one layer. Two if mature. Three is what shippable AI products do.
Measuring AI-Powered Product Outputs

How to build a Human Eval Rubric

  1. Dimensions — what you're scoring (accuracy, tone, completeness, safety, citation correctness)
  2. Scale — 1–5 or pass/fail, with anchor descriptions
  3. Examples — one example per scale point
  4. Disagreement protocol — what to do when graders disagree
  5. Sampling cadence — how often, how many

Real-world: Google Assistant uses a 5-point rubric across 4 dimensions, sampled at ~50 utterances/week per intent.

Speaker Notes

The rubric is a contract between the PM and the graders. Anchors per scale point are non-negotiable — without them, graders drift.

PM's Role in Evaluations

Eval is product surface

  • The PM owns the rubric and the golden set.
  • The PM defines the pass bar.
  • The PM decides the regression cadence.
  • The PM acts on eval data — model swap, data fix, architecture change.

If you delegate evals to QA, you have outsourced your product surface.

Speaker Notes

This is the most-pushed-back-on slide. Engineers want to own evals. They can run them, but the PM owns the bar.

AI Levers for Optimization

Three knobs when eval scores miss the bar

LeverTry firstCost
ModelSwap, upgrade, or fine-tuneLow (swap) to high (fine-tune)
DataImprove corpus, clean inputs, expand contextMid — your time + ops
ArchitectureAdd RAG, decompose agents, add guardrailsHigh — engineering time

Try model and data before architecture.

Speaker Notes

Most teams reach for architecture first. It's the most expensive and slowest. Use this rule of thumb to push back.
Operationalizing AI Risks

Governance framework — four buckets

  • Compliance — regulatory (GDPR, EU AI Act).
  • Safety — content, prompt injection, misuse.
  • Reliability — uptime, fallback paths, graceful degradation.
  • Reputation — public failure scenarios + response playbook.

Each bucket gets a row in your PM Execution Plan.

Speaker Notes

This is the same framework from M2. Difference: now you operationalise it with real metrics + owners.

Key takeaways

What you carry into Monday
