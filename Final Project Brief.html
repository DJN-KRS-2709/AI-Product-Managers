Final Project Brief — Juno PM

Across six modules you architect, spec, and ship an AI copilot — Juno PM — into a public GitHub repo. The repo is your portfolio. The repo is your certification submission.

What changed from the legacy brief. The previous course ran this as a 3–5 person group project with an in-class group demo. This course is individual. You commit your own artifacts to your own fork. The final showcase is async and optional. Grading happens on your repo, not on a slide deck.

The scenario

You are the AI PM at RocketShip, a hyper-growth B2B SaaS platform for Enterprise Data Teams. On paper, the company is winning. In reality, you are the bottleneck of the entire organisation. The team calls it the Signal Collapse:

  • Your inbox is a wall of P0 escalations.
  • Support has thousands of unread tickets.
  • Sales reports stalling because requirements can't be vetted fast enough.
  • You have an innovation budget but zero headcount.

Your bet: build Juno PM — an AI Associate PM that lives inside Slack, Notion, and Jira. Three pillars:

  • Synthesise insights — turn multi-channel chaos into structured, evidence-backed clarity.
  • Draft specs — produce Version-0.1 PRDs you refine instead of starting from a blank page.
  • Prioritise risks — flag unclear edge cases, technical debt, and risky assumptions.

The finalised README — your PM Execution Plan

The top-level README.md of your fork is the closing artifact. Generate it via Final Project Deliverables Builder.html. It must contain:

  1. Title + one-line pitch + name + cohort.
  2. Links to all six modules' artifacts.
  3. PM Execution Plan — Where Juno is today · What ships next (2 sprints) · What you watch (dashboards) · Red lines (numeric).
  4. Governance — one row per bucket (Compliance · Safety · Reliability · Reputation).
  5. Build Insights — 1 friction · 1 learning · 1 aha.
  6. (Optional) Loom URL — 3-min walkthrough of your repo.

How exercises run (individual format)

Every module's exercise follows the same pattern:

  1. Build. Use the module's interactive tool to produce the artifact.
  2. Self-review. Run the in-tool checklist (3–6 specific bullets).
  3. AI-review. Paste your artifact + the in-tool AI-review prompt into ChatGPT/Claude/Cursor. Reflect on the critique.
  4. Commit. Push the artifact to the named folder in your fork. Optionally post the link in #ai-pm-cohort.

No group breakouts. No partner work. No notetakers. Instructor responds in-thread within ~5 days for any post in #ai-pm-cohort.

Final showcase — async & optional

The original 30-minute live group showcase is replaced with an async, individual, optional walkthrough:

  1. Record a 3-minute Loom walkthrough of your repo. Hit: the bet, the system prompt, the user flow, the AWSpec, the eval stack.
  2. Post the repo URL + Loom in #ai-pm-cohort.
  3. Instructor responds in-thread within ~5 days with qualitative feedback.

Optional. Not required for certification. Encouraged for portfolio.

FAQs

Do I need to write code?

No. The interactive tools produce all artifacts. The optional M5 Langflow lab is the only place code-adjacent work happens, and we provide a starter export.

Do I have to do the Lovable prototype?

Yes — it's how you prove your M1 system prompt actually runs. Lovable's free tier is enough.

Do I have to do the optional showcase?

No. It doesn't affect certification. It is the highest-leverage portfolio asset to produce, however.

Can I submit a real product instead of Juno?

Only if you can map every artifact 1:1 to the Juno scenario and the grading is still possible on a public repo. Most learners get more out of Juno because every module's exercise is purpose-built for it.

What happens if I miss the 7-day window?

Submissions after the window may not be eligible for certification. Reach out via the dashboard if you have a genuine reason.
